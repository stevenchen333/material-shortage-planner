Data and planning assumptions

Order numbers, bike sizes, quantities and component usage come from the public AdventureWorks sample. The nine orders are grouped into one illustrative week. The stock counts, delivery dates, rush quote, capacity and late fees are scenario inputs.

A Road-150 uses one seat assembly. Four pinch bolts (PB-6109) go into that assembly. Monday opens with 12 bolts, four 44 cm frames and three 48 cm frames. Two 62 cm frames arrive on Thursday. The one inbound shipment contains 24 bolts. All other parts are ready.

The line builds up to two bikes per workday. Deliveries arrive before the shift and each bike ships on the day it is built. Early delivery is allowed. An order incurs its stated fee for each workday late. Monday’s releases are fixed. The due-date rule releases the earliest due order with a complete kit, breaking ties by order number.

Each policy takes whichever freight option gives it the lower total cost. The recovery model first minimizes late fees for each arrival, then preserves as many published dates as possible, then favors earlier releases. At an equal total cost, both policies keep the regular delivery.
